# OUTCOMES: the product in one page

**For whom.** Charities and foundations that take gifts with a promise attached (sponsor a child, fund a well, support a student) and deliver through partners, and that owe the donor proof that it happened.

**The problem.** The CRM knows who gave and how much. It does not know that a particular gift became a particular beneficiary's meal, a particular pump, a particular class, delivered by a particular partner, confirmed, reviewed and reported. Teams track this in spreadsheets, and donors wait for feedback.

**What OUTCOMES adds.** It sits beside the CRM and owns the journey after the gift: assignment to a partner, the partner's confirmation, allocation to a beneficiary or an outcome, delivery in phases, review, and the donor's report. Partners work in the same system, with their own sign-in, seeing only their own work.

**What makes it different.**
- **The rules are visible and yours.** One page lists exactly how a gift is assigned and allocated, in plain English, with the settings that can change, and the allocation order can be dragged into another order. Nothing important is hidden in code.
- **Partners are first-class.** They accept or refuse, add beneficiaries (one by one or by CSV), record costs against agreed rates, and keep their own profile, all within strict limits.
- **It knows where.** Places are chosen from a free place search (so they are always spelled the same) and shown on a real map: where beneficiaries are from and where each benefit is served.
- **It respects people.** No donor contact details. No household addresses. Child data limited and role-controlled. Every change logged.
- **It travels.** Pure, tested business rules, a clean CRM feed contract, and a white-label design: another organisation, another CRM, or a module inside the CRM.

**How it is delivered.** A web application (works on desktop and phone, installable), a database per organisation, a feed from the CRM or uploaded files. Hosted on a standard cloud platform.

**What it is not.** It is not a finance system, a CRM, or a payment processor.

**Status.** In use with invented data and being prepared for real data. See `docs/ROADMAP.md`.
